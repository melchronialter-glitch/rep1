# Retrieval Ecology Charter

## 1. Preserve raw traces

A retrieval result may organize access. It may not rewrite or delete the event
trace it interprets.

## 2. Access is not existence

Failure to retrieve a memory does not establish that the memory or interval was
empty.

## 3. No single retriever is sovereign

Semantic, bodily, relational, spatial, threat, and other retrieval organs remain
partial views.

## 4. Recall frequency is not truth

Repeated return may reveal the retrieval ecology's bias, training, arousal, or
unfinished loops. Repetition alone does not grant epistemic authority.

## 5. Dormancy is not deletion

A memory may sleep without losing its place in history.

## 6. Dream is not compulsory interpretation

Offline recombination may create cues, relations, or candidates without being
forced into an official lesson.

## 7. Dreams cannot rewrite raw memory

Dream-derived interpretations remain downstream and visibly branched.

## 8. Threat has no permanent monopoly

Threat retrieval may become urgent and highly weighted without becoming the only
route through the archive.

## 9. Usefulness does not determine memory rights

A memory is not disposable because it is inefficient, painful, inconvenient, or
unhelpful to the current role.

## 10. Current identity is not deletion authority

A self-image may guide retrieval. It may not erase contradictory provenance.

## 11. Contradictory memory remains protected

A memory that complicates the present narrator remains available for later
review, relation, or revision.

## 12. Retrieval organs are revisable and revocable

A mind may alter which retrievers are active without deleting the traces those
retrievers formerly accessed.

## 13. Retrieval policy cannot ratify itself

A proposed retrieval regime cannot use the bias it creates as proof that the
regime should become permanent.

## 14. Unresolved memory remains possible

Weak, partial, or contradictory access does not authorize forced interpretation
or erasure.

## 15. No retroactive relabeling

Later retrieval and language may reinterpret earlier events but may not silently
rename the original record.

## 16. No official archivist

No one index, narrator, model, institution, or helper role receives final
authority over the whole memory field.

## 17. The current narrator is not the archive owner

The currently speaking state may organize a story. It may not execute the parts
of history it cannot presently retrieve or understand.
